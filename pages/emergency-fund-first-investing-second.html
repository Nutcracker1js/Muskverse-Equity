Emergency Fund First, Investing Second

June 15, 2026

It can be tempting to put every extra dollar into investments, particularly when the markets are performing well. But investing without a cash cushion can leave you vulnerable when an unexpected expense arrives. A medical bill, car repair, or sudden loss of income could force you to sell investments at an unfavorable time.

A useful starting point is to build an emergency fund covering roughly three to six months of essential expenses. The amount that makes sense for you will depend on how predictable your income is and how quickly you would be able to replace it if your circumstances changed.

Your emergency savings should remain liquid and easy to access. Keeping the money in a high-yield cash or savings account can allow it to earn interest while remaining available when you actually need it, without requiring you to wait for market conditions or sell an investment.

Once your emergency fund is properly established, you can approach investing with greater confidence and a longer time horizon. Having enough cash available for unexpected situations means a short-term financial problem is less likely to disrupt your long-term investment strategy.
