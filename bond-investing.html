Don't Save Your Questions — We've Got Answers

What exactly is a bond ladder?

A bond ladder is a portfolio of bonds with staggered maturities, designed to reduce your exposure to interest rate swings. Each "rung" matures at a different interval — as bonds mature, you get your principal back to reinvest into existing or new rungs, offering predictable cash flow with less interest rate risk than holding a single bond.

No state income taxes on Treasuries? Really?

Really. Interest earned from Treasuries is exempt from state and local income taxes, so if you live somewhere with state income tax, you keep more of what you earn.

Is there a fee for withdrawing early?

No — unlike a CD, you can withdraw at any time. That said, you'll need to hold a Treasury to maturity to earn its full yield; selling early could mean losing some principal.

How do I know if this is right for me?

A bond ladder is designed for investors who want a steady yield with very little risk — a strong fit for saving toward a known expense, like a down payment or tuition, where capital preservation matters more than chasing higher returns.
